Contributions#

I conducted the user study alongside a partner, and I wrote the notes available in the document.

Description#

To evaluate our app, a partner and I conducted a user study where we gave the participant basic context about the app in advance, that it was an invite-based app that linked to their calendar and lets them choose which campus events to hear from. We then watched them walk through the onboarding process without interfering, letting them interact with the app naturally while I noted where they paused or commented. After they finished, we followed up with questions about suggestions and things they’d want added.

The participant moved through the onboarding without major issues, which validated that the interface was intuitive and simple enough for a first-time user to navigate on their own. There was a small confusion about a button related to the app’s color theme, so we noted that we should make it clearer. The follow-up conversation gave useful ideas for future development. They suggested adding more broad filters such as a “Food” filter so users could hear about any event with food regardless of which organization is hosting it, which would be helpful especially for students on limited meal plans (saving money on meal swipes). They also brought up the idea of an event suggestions feature that would look at a user’s free time on their calendar and recommend upcoming events that fit. One idea the user brought up that may have seemed obvious but that we hadn’t thought about before was to send event invites on a weekly basis, but send course invites only once per semester. Implementing this would make the experience much less overwhelming for users by reducing the redundant class invites while also reducing unnecessary scraping. The interview showed us that the core app worked well, and that the main opportunities for improvement were around personalization and smarter scheduling of how and when invites are delivered.
