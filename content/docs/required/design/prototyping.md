# Prototyping

## Sample

“Prototype” folder in the group Google Drive, linked in the “team-wiki” repository.

## Contributions

I participated in the group discussions that shaped the prototype design. The sketches were drawn by one other teammate on their iPad. I also took part in the prototype presentation and in asking people for feedback on our sketches.

## Description

The main design questions we wanted to answer with our prototype were what the interface would look like and how users would respond to the event selection and preference setup. Since we were building something meant to be easy to set up, we needed to know whether the interface felt intuitive or overwhelming to a new user. We therefore intentionally kept the prototype design simple by limiting the amount of information on the screen at once, organizing the information into dropdown menus, and separating everything into only three tabs (Schedule, Hours, Preferences). We sketched a paper prototype covering the onboarding flow and the main page, showing what each of the three tabs would look like. One teammate then separately put the sketch together on his iPad. 

We presented it to the class to gather feedback, and I also showed it to two people I had previously interviewed during the Needfinding phase, which gave a useful before and after perspective from people who had already shared their ideas with us. Using a paper prototype at this stage let us test the overall look and layout before writing any frontend code. The feedback was mainly positive, with users liking the simplicity of the interface. The one area that needed improvement was the "send me invites at 5:00 AM" section on the preferences tab, which was our way of letting users choose when they receive their weekly event invites. Users felt that it wasn't immediately clear what that setting did, so we noted that the label needed to be more descriptive to avoid confusion during onboarding.
